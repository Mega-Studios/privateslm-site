SLM Daily #17: one tensor op was making 65,536 memcpy calls of four bytes each

August 24, 2026 · 5 min read

A single tensor operation in llama.cpp was issuing 65,536 separate memcpy calls of four bytes each. Someone replaced them with row copies on August 22, and the same operation dropped from 1.61 ms to 0.012 ms — 133x. That's the good news since our last edition (2026-08-22T04:45 UTC), spanning build tags b10570 through b10603. The less good news landed the next day: twenty-two vision models turned out to have been preprocessing images the wrong way for months.

The concat that was copying four bytes at a time

PR #24575, merged August 22, rewrote ggml's concat operation. The old code walked the tensor element by element, issuing one memcpy per element — 65,536 calls of four bytes each on the shape the author benchmarked. The new code collapses the innermost loop and copies whole rows.

On a [4096 × 1 × 16 × 1] fp32 concat along dim 2, measured on an M1 Air over 200 runs:

ConditionBeforeAfterSpeedup
Warm cache1.61 ms/call0.012 ms/call133x
Cold cache1.59 ms/call0.019 ms/call83x

Source: ggml-org/llama.cpp#24575, author's own microbenchmark. Note this is an isolated op benchmark, not end-to-end generation — the PR makes no claim about tokens/sec, and neither do we.

The caveat matters: a 133x win on one operator is not a 133x win on inference. How much you feel depends on how much wall-clock that operator owned to begin with, and nobody has published that split — not the PR, and not us.

22 vision models were being resized wrong

PR #27594, merged August 23 (tag b10598), fixed something more interesting than a speedup: llama.cpp's multimodal path had been using the wrong image-resize algorithm for more than twenty models, relative to the Pillow-based preprocessing those models were actually trained with.

LLaVA 1.5/1.6, MobileVLM, CogVLM2, Qwen2-VL, GLM-4.xV, Pixtral and Janus Pro were resizing BILINEAR where Hugging Face uses BICUBIC. Idefics3/SmolVLM and HunyuanOCR should have been LANCZOS. The images still loaded, the models still answered, and the embeddings quietly diverged from what training produced. No error, no warning — the exact failure signature we keep writing about, most recently in #13's zero-padded LoRA files. The corrected BICUBIC path is also faster: 4.8x on a 4032×3024 → 1024×1024 downscale, and 53x on a 336×336 → 3072×3072 upscale, both against the legacy implementation.

This doesn't touch our own users — privateSLM has no code path that hands a model an image, which is the same reason we skipped LFM2.5-VL-3B back in #11. We're flagging it because "silently trained one way, silently run another" is a category of bug worth recognizing on sight.

A third fix, PR #26756 (b10593), repaired multi-sequence rollback for DeepSeek-V4, where parallel decoding plus speculation corrupted compressed KV state across ubatches: "occasional segfault during state-restore at second rollback" before, "clean across 30+ prefill rounds" after. Server-class model; the bug class isn't.

New architectures: none you can hold

Follow-up work landed for bailingmoe3 (Ant Group's Ling 3.0) including DSpark speculative decoding, which took prediction throughput from 44.20 to 55.73 tok/s on a DGX Spark. Even Ling-3.0-tiny is 7.9B total parameters with 1.3B active — a mixture-of-experts model whose full weights still have to live in memory, so "tiny" here isn't phone-tiny. dots3-note, whose text architecture we covered in #16, picked up vision and audio support; it remains 280B parameters. GLM-4.5-Air gained multi-token prediction at 106B. Nothing sub-4B arrived.

Three candidates checked, zero added

Liquid AI published quantization-aware distillation builds of its two smallest models on August 19. We verified both by HTTP HEAD against the vendor's own repo: LFM2.5-350M-QAD-Q4_0.gguf is 219,312,832 bytes (209 MB) and LFM2.5-230M-QAD-Q4_0.gguf is 149,081,056 bytes (142 MB). Both are vendor-quantized, both carry the LFM Open License v1.0 we already accept, and both run on the lfm2 architecture our engine supports.

The numbers are interesting. Liquid's QAD write-up reports the 350M build retaining 96.5% of its BF16 baseline across seven evals, and the 230M build 97.1% — which sits awkwardly beside the retention curve we published six days ago, where a 0.6B model kept 70.8% at 4-bit. Different eval set, and crucially a quantization method applied during training rather than after it. We're noting the tension, not resolving it.

Neither enters the catalog today. Our Generalists tier is full at eight, and our rules only allow a replacement when a newcomer strictly dominates an incumbent. A 350M model doesn't — it's a different tier of capability, aimed at hardware well below an iPhone. Adding it would mean breaking our own cap or evicting a better model.

Also checked and out on size: Qwen3.8-27B, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B and GLM-5.2, all far past our 2.5 GB ceiling even before quantization tricks. Catalog stays at 8 Generalists and 14 specialists.
