SLM Daily #18: This week's smallest 'small' model needs 21.39 GB. Our whole catalog fits in 14.

August 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Thomson Reuters calls its new model "Small." At Q4_K_M — the exact quant tier our own catalog ships as — it's 21.39 GB, bigger on its own than all eight models in our Generalists tier added together (13.97 GB, computed straight from our own catalog's sizeBytes fields). It isn't the only "small"-branded release this week that's actually enormous — a second lab shipped a "mini" the same week. Roughly a day since SLM Daily #17 (2026-08-27T04:18 UTC): two labs, one naming irony, and a new llama.cpp architecture with an embedding table alone bigger than our whole catalog. Zero catalog changes, zero DIY spotlight — here's the full accounting.

This week, "small" means 35 billion parameters

Thomson Reuters launched its first proprietary LLM family, Thomson 1.0, on August 24 (Law.com). Thomson-1.0-Small is the open-weight member: continued pre-training of Alibaba's Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on Thomson's own Westlaw, Practical Law, and news archives, per the model's own Hugging Face card. "Small" describes its place in the Thomson lineup, not its footprint: 35B total parameters, 3B active per token (mixture-of-experts), a 262,144-token context window. We verified bartowski's GGUF directly — Q4_K_M lists at 21.39 GB, 8.6x our own 2.5 GB per-model phone cap. It also ships under the Polyform Strict License 1.0.0, a non-commercial, no-redistribution license that would keep it out of our catalog even at a phone-sized file.

The same week, a second lab did almost the same thing. Apodex AI's Apodex-1.1-mini (also dated August 24, per its own launch post) is 36B parameters — confirmed directly from its own config.json: model_type: qwen3_5_moe, 256 experts, 8 active per token, built on a different Qwen base (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B). Its license, Apache 2.0, is one we'd actually take. But it's also multimodal — a real vision_config block with dedicated image and video token IDs — the same gap that's kept every VLM out of our catalog since SLM Daily #11, independent of size. The repo's own BF16 safetensors sum to 71.9 GB; no GGUF has landed on Apodex's own page as of today.

The engine: an embedding table bigger than our whole catalog

llama.cpp picked up a third oversized release this week too. Tag b10660 (merged August 27, 20:02 UTC, PR #27742) adds a new qwen4exp architecture for Qwen's own Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. Per Qwen's own model card: a 125B-parameter MoE backbone with 6B active per token, plus a separate 51B-parameter "n-gram embedding" table and a 4B multi-token-prediction module bolted on. llama.cpp's own release notes single out that embedding table on its own — 26.8 GiB once quantized to IQ4_NL, more than double the combined size of every model in our Generalists tier added together (13.97 GB, all eight, Q4_K_M or nearest). None of it is remotely phone-sized and none of it touches our catalog, but it's a genuinely new architecture in the engine we vendor, so it's on the record.

Zero catalog changes, zero DIY spotlight

Nothing we checked this window cleared the size cap, the license gate, or the engine-compatibility gate — Thomson and Apodex above are the only two candidates that got as far as a real, HEAD-verified GGUF or a real config.json before falling out. On the DIY side, the one build we found with real hardware and real numbers this window — a Qualcomm Dragonwing IQ-9075 running a two-stage hand-tracking pipeline, palm detection plus 21-point landmark tracking, both quantized to int8 and offloaded to the board's Hexagon NPU — doesn't run a language model at all. It's a computer-vision pipeline, not an SLM, so it's outside what this spotlight covers, however real the hardware is. Nothing else this window had both a verifiable source and a genuine on-device-AI angle we hadn't already run.
